Living with concrete in Florida

Concrete is one of the easiest planter materials to own here. A few notes so yours looks good for decades.

Watering and drainage

Concrete breathes a little, so soil dries more evenly than in plastic and roots are less likely to rot in a wet summer. Water when the top inch of soil is dry and keep the drainage hole clear; a handful of gravel at the bottom helps.

Sun, rain and storms

Florida sun will not fade the natural gray, and rain runs straight through the drainage hole. The weight is the real feature: a large bowl or cylinder stays put in the kind of wind that sends plastic pots down the street.

Patina and cleaning

Over the first year the surface softens and lightens slightly, which most people like. To clean, use water and a stiff brush. Skip pressure washers and acid cleaners.

Choosing a size

Pick a planter about two inches wider than the plant’s current pot. The 15-inch bowl suits succulents and herbs, the 24-inch bowl takes a small shrub, and the 27-inch cylinder will hold a palm or a tall grass. A set of three nested bowls reads well beside a front door; a pair of cylinders frames a gate or a corner of the lanai.
